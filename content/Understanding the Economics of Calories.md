# Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)

When we talk about daily calorie burn, we are looking at your **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**. Your TDEE is made up of four distinct components:

1. **BMR**: Resting energy expenditure
2. **NEAT**: Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis
3. **EAT**: Exercise Activity Thermogenesis
4. **TEF**: Thermic Effect of Food

## Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)

This is the amount of energy our body needs to maintain our body structure.

- The bigger the body (along with age, sex, and some other physiological factors), the bigger the energy needed to maintain it.
- **The Car Analogy**: Think of it like a car—the bigger and more expensive your car is, the higher its maintenance cost rises.
- **How to increase BMR?** Simply make your car bigger **and** expensive.

Our body contains two main dynamic tissues that can grow or shrink considerably during our lifetime:

- <mark style="background-color: rgba(76, 175, 80, 0.25); color: inherit; padding: 2px 4px; border-radius: 4px;">Muscle Tissue</mark>: The more metabolically active tissue per kilogram.
- <mark style="background-color: rgba(244, 67, 54, 0.25); color: inherit; padding: 2px 4px; border-radius: 4px;">Adipose Tissue</mark>: The less metabolically active tissue per kilogram.

Here, the muscle tissue is the **luxury component** of your body. It raises the value of parts of your car and thus its maintenance cost.

> [!quote] The Terry Jeffords Example
> Have you watched the show _Brooklyn 99_? Terry Jeffords used to eat more than Scully and his partner, yet he was less fat. He used to say he needed **10,000 calories** just to maintain his muscle mass. If he ate less than that, his muscles would not exist anymore.

## Thermic Effect of Food (TEF)

TEF is the amount of energy your body uses to digest, absorb, and process the nutrients in the diet you eat.

The calories you eat don't reach your body to be used as such—some of these calories are spent just moving them from the outside to the inside.

Among the major macros, **protein has the highest thermic effect** (almost twice to thrice that of carbohydrates). For carbohydrates, the more complex they are, the higher the thermic effect.

### Macronutrient Breakdown

| Macro             | Estimated TEF                                                    |
| :---------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Protein**       | <span style="color: #4CAF50; font-weight: bold;">20 – 30%</span> |
| **Carbohydrates** | <span style="color: #FF9800; font-weight: bold;">5 – 10%</span>  |
| **Fats**          | <span style="color: #F44336; font-weight: bold;">0 – 3%</span>   |

### Protein Energy Stages

| Energy Stage             | Description                                                                                                                 | Value                |
| :----------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------- |
| **Gross Energy**         | The total raw energy inside the food (measured in a laboratory bomb calorimeter).                                           | `~5.65 kcal/g`       |
| **Net Available Energy** | The energy absorbed by your body after accounting for undigested food and nitrogen waste in urine (the 4 kcal/g on labels). | `4.0 kcal/g`         |
| **Metabolizable Energy** | The actual energy left over for your cells to use after subtracting the energy spent on digestion (TEF).                    | `~2.8 to 3.2 kcal/g` |

### TEF in Staple Foods

| Staple Food          | Estimated TEF |
| :------------------- | :------------ |
| **Steel Cut Oats**   | `10 – 12%`    |
| **Brown Rice**       | `8 – 10%`     |
| **Whole Wheat Roti** | `8 – 10%`     |
| **White Bread**      | `5 – 6%`      |
| **White Rice**       | `5%`          |

> [!tip] Fat Loss Strategy
> If you get most of your calories from **protein** and **complex carbs**, it will be significantly better for your fat loss journey.

## Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (NEAT)

> [!important] The Strongest Weapon
> NEAT is the strongest weapon for daily energy expenditure, yet it is almost always ignored.

Let's look at the weekly time breakdown:

<div style="background-color: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.05); padding: 15px; border-radius: 8px; border-left: 4px solid #2196F3; margin-bottom: 10px;">

- **Total Hours in a Week**: `168 Hours`
- **Sleep (8 hrs/day)**: `-56 Hours` &rarr; `112 Hours Left`
- **Exercise (1 hr/day, 6 days/wk)**: `-6 Hours` &rarr; <span style="color: #2196F3; font-weight: bold;">106 Hours Left</span>

</div>

How you spend those **106 hours** matters immensely. You could stay sedentary, or you can actively optimize NEAT:

- Walk frequently
- Stand more
- Take the stairs
- Walk after meals
- Commute actively

Because NEAT accumulates over many hours throughout the day, the total energy spent can become substantial.

## Exercise Activity Thermogenesis (EAT)

Planned physical activities contribute to total energy output based on activity type and intensity:

| Exercise                                  | Intensity       | Energy Spent (`kcal/hour`) |
| :---------------------------------------- | :-------------- | :------------------------- |
| **Yoga, gentle**                          | Low             | ~150                       |
| **Walking, easy (3 km/h)**                | Low             | ~150 – 220                 |
| **Yoga, vigorous**                        | Low – Moderate  | ~200 – 350                 |
| **Walking, brisk (5–6 km/h)**             | Moderate        | ~250 – 350                 |
| **Cycling, easy**                         | Moderate        | ~300 – 400                 |
| **Resistance training**                   | Moderate        | ~300 – 450                 |
| **Jogging (8 km/h)**                      | Moderate        | ~350 – 550                 |
| **Elliptical**                            | Moderate        | ~400 – 550                 |
| **Swimming, moderate**                    | Moderate        | ~400 – 650                 |
| **Cycling, moderate**                     | Moderate        | ~450 – 600                 |
| **Rowing, moderate**                      | Moderate – Hard | ~450 – 600                 |
| **Hiking**                                | Moderate – Hard | ~450 – 600                 |
| **Running (10 km/h)**                     | Hard            | ~550                       |
| **Rowing, vigorous**                      | Hard            | ~600 – 800                 |
| **Running (12 km/h)**                     | Hard            | ~600 – 800                 |
| **Swimming, vigorous**                    | Hard            | ~600 – 800                 |
| **Cycling, vigorous**                     | Hard            | ~650 – 800                 |
| **Resistance training, vigorous/circuit** | Hard            | ~650 – 850                 |
| **Stair climbing**                        | Hard            | ~700                       |
| **Jump rope**                             | Hard            | ~700 – 900                 |
| **HIIT**                                  | Very Hard       | ~500 – 900*                |

<span style="font-size: 0.85em; opacity: 0.8;">_\*Vigorous activities like running, stair climbing, jump rope, and HIIT represent peak calorie expenditure per hour._</span>
